Discussion

General

According to ChatGPT, z-scores, also known as standard scores, are a statistical measure that indicates how many standard deviations a data point is from the mean of the dataset. They are a way to standardize data so that it can be compared across different datasets with different scales and means.

The formula for calculating a z-score is:

z = (x - μ) / σ

Where:

  • x is the value you want to convert to a z-score
  • μ is the mean of the dataset
  • σ is the standard deviation of the dataset

Once you calculate the z-score, you can interpret it as follows:

  • A positive z-score indicates that the data point is above the mean.
  • A negative z-score indicates that the data point is below the mean.
  • The absolute value of the z-score indicates how far the data point is from the mean in terms of standard deviations.

For example, let's say you have a dataset of exam scores with a mean of 70 and a standard deviation of 10. A student scored an 85 on the exam. To calculate the z-score for this score, you would use the formula:

z = (85 - 70) / 10 = 1.5

This means that the student's score is 1.5 standard deviations above the mean.

Z-scores can be used for a variety of purposes, such as identifying outliers, comparing data from different datasets, and determining the probability of a data point occurring within a certain range.

Computing Probability

Continuing with ChatGPT, to compute the probability of a data point occurring within a certain range using z-scores, you can use a standard normal distribution table or statistical software.

The standard normal distribution is a probability distribution that has a mean of 0 and a standard deviation of 1. The z-score of any data point can be transformed into a corresponding standard normal distribution value using a table or software.

For example, suppose we want to find the probability of a data point being less than or equal to a certain value x, given a dataset with a mean of μ and a standard deviation of σ. We can use the z-score formula to convert x into a z-score:

z = (x - μ) / σ

Once we have the z-score, we can use a standard normal distribution table or software to find the probability of a data point being less than or equal to that z-score. The table or software will give us a probability value, which we can interpret as the probability of a data point being less than or equal to x.

For example, let's say we have a dataset of heights with a mean of 68 inches and a standard deviation of 3 inches. We want to find the probability of a person being less than or equal to 70 inches tall. We can use the z-score formula to convert 70 inches into a z-score:

z = (70 - 68) / 3 = 0.67

We can then use a standard normal distribution table or software to find the probability of a data point being less than or equal to a z-score of 0.67, which is approximately 0.7486. This means that there is a 74.86% chance of a person being less than or equal to 70 inches tall in this dataset.

Example Probability Computation

Continuing with ChatGPT, assume

μ = 399.32
σ = 66.59
Q1 = 352.62

What is the probability that x < Q1

To find the probability that x is less than Q1, we need to calculate the z-score for Q1 and then use a standard normal distribution table or calculator to find the corresponding probability.

The z-score formula is:

z = (x - μ) / σ

Substituting the values given:

z = (352.62 - 399.32) / 66.59
z = -0.703

Using a standard normal distribution table or calculator, we find that the probability of z being less than -0.703 is approximately 0.2419.

Therefore, the probability that x is less than Q1 is 0.2419 or about 24.19%.

The reason why the probability is not exactly 0.25 as you might expect is because the standard normal distribution is a continuous probability distribution, meaning that it assigns probabilities to ranges of values rather than specific values.

In this case, the z-score of -0.703 corresponds to a range of values between negative infinity and -0.703 on the standard normal distribution curve. The area under the curve for this range is approximately 0.241, which represents the probability of observing a value less than Q1.

While the probability of observing a value less than Q1 is close to 0.25, it is not exactly 0.25 because the normal distribution is a continuous probability distribution, and probabilities are assigned to ranges of values rather than specific values.
